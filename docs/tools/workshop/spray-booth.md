An extracted booth for spray paint, primer, lacquer, solvent cleaning and anything else that
puts a vapour into the air. Also where resin work and any solvent decanting happens.

:::danger[The hazard is vapour, not paint]
Solvent vapour is heavier than air, it pools, and it is flammable well below the
concentration at which it smells strongly. That is why the rules are about extraction and
ignition rather than about the spraying.
:::

times, waste and rag disposal, and the fire case.

:::info[Needs a real answer]
Duration and who gives it. Also whether the space provides respirators or expects members to

face, so there is a real decision here.
:::

## Before you start

1. **Extraction on, and confirmed pulling.** Tissue at the filter face. Not "the fan is on" —
   filters clog and a clogged booth moves no air.
2. **Respirator on and sealed**, with the right cartridge for organic vapour. A dust mask does
   nothing against solvent. Clean-shaven at the seal, or it does not seal.
3. **Eye protection**, and gloves appropriate to the solvent — nitrile is not universal; some
   solvents go straight through it.
4. **Nothing hot, sparking or live in the room.** No soldering, no grinding, no bench PSU, no
   3D printer running, no laser, no vape. Nothing on an extension lead trailing in.
5. **Read the label on what you are using**, every time, including the can you have used
   twenty times. Know what the solvent is.
6. **Tell someone you are in there**, and when you expect to be out.
7. **Door shut while spraying**, so the extraction pulls through the booth rather than the
   workshop.

## Known quirks

- **Flash-off is longer than you think**, and it is when most of the vapour comes off — not
  during spraying. Leave the extraction running for the time the induction gives you after
  you finish, with the door shut.
- **Solvent-soaked rags can self-heat and ignite.** Linseed oil and some polyurethanes are the
  worst. They go in the lidded metal bin, not the general waste, not balled up on the bench.
- **Clogged filters are the usual cause of a booth that "smells".** Change is a maintainer
  job; the smell is your cue to report, not to carry on with the door open.
- **Cold, damp days ruin finishes.** A London winter workshop will bloom lacquer. Warm the
  part, not the booth.
- **Two-pack and isocyanate-containing products are not permitted.** They need
  air-fed breathing apparatus and health surveillance, which the space does not have. This
  includes most automotive 2K clearcoats and some polyurethane kits — check the label for
  isocyanate before you bring it.
- **Resin printing and resin casting go in here**, not at a bench. Uncured resin is a
  sensitiser: get sensitised once and you are done with resin for life.

:::warning[If someone is overcome]
Get them out into fresh air — do not go in holding your breath to drag them out if the air is
visibly thick; the whole point of the extraction rule is to avoid two casualties. 999,
tell them it is a solvent exposure, and log it. See
[Incidents & first aid](../../safety/incidents-and-first-aid.md).
:::

**Everything flammable goes back in the flammables cabinet** at the end of your session.
Nothing is left out in the booth, nothing goes to your locker, nothing goes on a shelf.

:::info[Needs a real answer]
The maximum quantity of flammable liquid the space may store, and what the cabinet is rated
for. This is a legal limit, not a preference, and it needs a real figure from whoever did the
building's fire risk assessment. Until it is answered, keep quantities to what fits in the
cabinet with room to spare.
:::

## If it breaks

Nothing is safe to try. If extraction is not pulling, the booth is out of service — tag it
and tell the maintainer. A spray booth with weak extraction is more dangerous than no spray
booth, because people still use it.
