A 60 W sealed CO₂ tube, water-cooled, with air assist and ducted extraction. Cuts ply,
acrylic, card, leather, cork and fabric; engraves anodised aluminium and coated metals. It
does not cut bare metal.

:::danger[The two rules that have no exceptions]
**1. Never leave it running.** Not for a minute. A laser fire goes from a flare to a machine
fire in well under a minute, in a room with dust and plywood in it. You stand there, where
you can see the work and reach the lid and the stop.

**2. Never cut PVC, vinyl, PTFE, polycarbonate or anything you cannot identify.** PVC and
vinyl release hydrogen chloride, which is immediately dangerous to you and destroys the
machine's optics and metalwork from the inside. There is no extraction setting that makes
this safe.
:::

## Material rules

| Cut | Engrave only | **Never** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plywood, MDF | Anodised aluminium | PVC, vinyl, pleather on PVC |
| Cast acrylic (PMMA) | Coated/painted metal | PTFE and anything fluorinated |
| Card, paper, mountboard | Glass | Polycarbonate (Lexan) |
| Leather (veg-tan) | Slate, stone | ABS |
| Cork, felt, cotton | | Fibreglass, carbon fibre |
| | | Any mirrored or metallised sheet |
| | | **Anything unlabelled** |

Why the "never" column: PVC and PTFE give off acid gas; polycarbonate and ABS burn, smoke and
leave a brown edge instead of cutting; glass fibre and carbon fibre release fibres and do not
cut; mirrored sheet reflects the beam back into the machine. Unlabelled sheet is in the list
because you cannot tell acrylic from polycarbonate by looking.

:::note[Testing an unknown sheet]
Do not test it in the laser. Ask the maintainer. The copper-wire Beilstein test for chlorine
is a chemistry-lab procedure, not a workshop one — if the sheet has no label and no provenance
it does not go in the machine.
:::

## Before you start

1. **Extraction on and confirmed pulling** — feel it at the bed, do not just hear the fan.
2. **Chiller running and at temperature.** Cutting with a warm tube shortens its life sharply.
3. **Air assist on.** Without it the cut smoulders instead of cutting and the flare risk goes up.
4. **Material identified and on the list.** Labelled, or you know its provenance.
5. **Focus set** for the material thickness, with the gauge.
6. **Bed clear of offcuts** from the last person. Small offcuts under the work are what
   catches fire.
7. **Test cut on a scrap corner** at your settings before committing a full sheet.
8. **Fire blanket and CO₂ extinguisher located** before you start, not during.
9. **Lid closed.** The interlock is there because the beam is invisible and blinding.

## Known quirks

- **A small flame at the cut line is normal; a flame that travels with the head is not.** The
  second one means too slow, too much power, or no air assist. Stop.
- **Acrylic flames look alarming and usually self-extinguish; plywood smoulders quietly and
  does not.** The dangerous fire is the one you cannot see, in the honeycomb under the work.
- **Mirrors and the lens need checking, not just cleaning.** Cut quality falling off across
  one side of the bed is nearly always optics, not power. Tell the maintainer rather than
  winding the power up — more power through a dirty lens cracks the lens.
- **Cut power is not linear and ages.** A tube at the end of its life needs noticeably more
  power for the same cut. Settings from a year-old project may not reproduce.
- **MDF stinks and dulls the machine faster than ply.** Allowed, but duct it properly and
  expect to clean up after yourself.
- **Leather must be veg-tan.** Chrome-tanned leather releases chromium compounds.

:::warning[If it catches fire]
Lid stays **closed**. Power off at the wall. Fire blanket over the machine, or CO₂ into the
bed through the smallest opening you can manage. Never water — water into a live
high-voltage machine. Then 999, then Telegram. Log it either way, even if you put it out in
ten seconds — see [Incidents & first aid](../../safety/incidents-and-first-aid.md).
:::

## If it breaks

Nothing is safe to try. No opening panels, no touching optics, no adjusting the chiller, and
absolutely no bypassing an interlock that is "being annoying" — that interlock is the only
thing between the beam and someone's retina.

Off at the wall, tag it, Telegram, maintainer.
