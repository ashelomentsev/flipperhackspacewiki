A 550 W handheld hot air gun, 100–450 °C, with all controls and a colour screen on the
handle. The stand senses the gun magnetically: dock it and it blows cool air, then sleeps.

Hot air is for removing and fitting SMD parts, for anything with no accessible leads, and for
heat-shrink.

## How to use

1. **Fit a nozzle while it's cold.** 850-series nozzles; spares on the steel plate beside the
   stand.
2. Press the square power button. Use **◀ ▶** to change temperature or airflow, **■** to switch
   between them or pick one of the three presets.
3. Starting points: **300–350 °C, airflow 8–12** for small SMD parts. Heat-shrink: about
   **200 °C**.
4. Keep the nozzle moving, 1–2 cm above the board. Shield nearby plastic parts with Kapton
   tape.
5. Dock it in the stand. **Wait for the cool-down to finish before unplugging.**

## Don't

- **Don't point it at people.**
- **Don't use it near the solder spools or the plastic bins.** It will melt them from further
  away than you expect.
- **Don't touch the nozzle for several minutes after use.** It stays dangerously hot with
  nothing to show it. Into the stand, every time.
- **Don't blow hot air at the [Microscope Camera](./microscope-camera.md) lens.** It damages
  the optics.

## Known quirks

- **High airflow blows small parts across the room.** 0402s especially. Low flow, high
  temperature, close nozzle — not the other way round.
- **Everything within about 10 mm of the nozzle is coming with you.** Kapton-tape the
  neighbours down before you start.

## Consumables

850-series nozzles on the steel plate beside the stand. Kapton tape on the
bench shelf.

## If it breaks

Safe to try: power cycle, check the nozzle is seated.

A gun that won't reach temperature, whose fan sounds wrong, or that smells of burning gets
unplugged after its cool-down and tagged.
