# Flipper

Flipper Zero is made upstairs. That is a genuine advantage of being a member here — the people
who wrote the firmware come down for coffee — and it is also why this section leads with a
policy page rather than a tutorial.

## Read this first

**[Responsible use](./responsible-use.md).**

Not a formality. The Flipper touches sub-GHz radio, NFC, RFID, infrared and access control, and
in the UK most of what it can do is regulated, some of it criminally. The policy page is short,
it is binding on members, and it protects three things at once: you, the space, and the brand
of the company whose office is on floor 03.

If you read one page in this section, read that one.

## What the Flipper is actually for, here

It is a pocket multi-tool for digital interfaces, and the interesting use at a hackspace is
**understanding your own hardware** — reading a protocol you implemented, checking what your
board is emitting, driving a bus from the GPIO, standing in for a logic probe when you are away
from the [bench](../tools/electronics/index.md).

It is a genuinely good tool for that. It is a poor tool for the thing people buy it for, and
the policy page explains why.

## Asking the team

The Flipper Devices team is on floor 03 and the membership includes bumping into them. Worth
knowing how to use that well:

- **Good:** "here's my board, it browns out when the sub-GHz TX fires, any idea why?"
- **Good:** "is this protocol documented anywhere, or did I find a gap?"
- **Less good:** treating them as a firmware helpdesk for a device you bought.
- **Not on:** asking them how to do something the [policy](./responsible-use.md) rules out. They
  will say no, and it puts them in an awkward position in their own office.
