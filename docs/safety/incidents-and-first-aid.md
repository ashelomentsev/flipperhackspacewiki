# Incidents & first aid

## Right now

**Serious injury, fire, or someone unconscious or confused: 999 first.** Tell the Telegram
second. Nobody here has ever been criticised for calling an ambulance that turned out not to be
needed.

| | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| **Fire** | Everyone out, pull the alarm, 999. Only fight a fire you are sure of, with your back to the exit |
| **Burn** | Cool running water, 20 minutes, properly. Not ice, not butter, don't peel anything off. 999 if it is larger than the palm of a hand, or on the face or hands |
| **Cut** | Pressure, elevate. 999 if it spurts, won't stop, or you can see what you shouldn't |
| **Electric shock** | **Don't touch them** until the supply is off. Then 999, even if they look fine — cardiac effects are delayed |
| **Eye injury or chemical in the eye** | Irrigate for 15 minutes minimum, then A&E. Take the product label with you |
| **Solvent exposure, dizziness** | Fresh air. Don't enter visibly thick air to drag someone out — two casualties is worse. 999 |
| **Caught in a machine** | Emergency stop, don't reverse the machine, 999. Don't try to free someone by force |

## Everything gets logged

**Every injury. Every near miss. Every fire you put out in ten seconds. Every machine that did
something unexpected.**

:::info[Needs a real answer]
Where. A paper accident book in the lobby, a form, a Telegram channel — it needs to be a real
place, and someone needs to read it.
:::

### Why near misses matter more than injuries

The near miss is the same event with better luck. A board that caught fire in the laser and went
out on its own is the same event as the one that didn't, and it is the only warning you get for
free.

So: log the thing you got away with. Log the thing that was your own fault. Log the thing that
makes you look careless.

**Nobody is in trouble for reporting.** The only reportable offences here are the three on
[House rules](../start-here/house-rules.md), and none of them is "had an accident". What does
get a membership looked at is hiding a fault that then hurt the next person.

## What goes in the log

Enough that someone who was not there can work out what to change:

- What you were doing, and on which machine.
- What happened, in plain words.
- What stopped it getting worse — luck included, honestly.
- What you think would prevent it. This is the useful field.
- Date, time, and who was in the building.

## RIDDOR

Some workplace incidents must be reported to the HSE by law — specified injuries, dangerous
occurrences, and injuries causing more than seven days off work.

:::warning[Needs a competent person, not a wiki page]
Who holds the RIDDOR duty for this space, and how a reportable incident reaches them, needs
settling by Flipper Technologies Ltd. **This page is not a substitute for that**, and a member
should never be left working out whether something is RIDDOR-reportable at 3am.

Report it to the space; let the space work out its legal obligations.
:::

## After an incident

1. **Make it safe.** Tag the machine out of service — see
   [Tools & equipment](../tools/index.md#when-a-tool-is-broken).
2. **Log it**, while you remember it.
3. **Tell the maintainer and the Telegram**, so the next person does not walk into it.
4. **Update the tool page** if you learned something that belongs in its known quirks. That is
   how this wiki gets good — see [Editing this wiki](../contributing.md).
