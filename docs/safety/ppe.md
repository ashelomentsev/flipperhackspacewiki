# PPE

Personal protective equipment is the **last** line, not the first. A guard, an interlock or a
better method beats PPE every time — PPE is what is left when the hazard cannot be removed.

## By job

| Doing this | Wear |
| --- | --- |
| Soldering, clipping leads, hot air | [Safety glasses](../tools/electronics/safety-glasses.md) |
| Handling bare boards | ESD wrist strap, clipped to the [mat](../tools/electronics/esd-mat.md) |
| [CNC mill](../tools/workshop/cnc-mill.md) | Eye protection. **No gloves** — see below |
| [Laser cutter](../tools/workshop/laser-cutter-co2.md) | Lid closed is the protection. No laser goggles needed with the lid shut |
| [Spray booth](../tools/workshop/spray-booth.md) | Respirator with organic-vapour cartridge, eye protection, gloves matched to the solvent |
| Resin printing or casting | Nitrile gloves, eye protection, booth extraction |
| Anything loud — mill, compressor, grinder | Hearing protection |
| Sweeping or vacuuming fine dust | Dust mask, and wet it down if you can |

## The two places instinct is wrong

:::danger[No gloves at the mill]
Gloves make rotating machinery **more** dangerous, not less. A glove catches on a cutter and
pulls your hand in; bare skin tears and lets go. Along with gloves: no loose sleeves, no rings,
no lanyard, no tie, long hair tied back.

This is the injury that takes a finger, and it is nearly always entanglement rather than a cut.
:::

:::danger[A dust mask does nothing against solvent]
The paper masks are for particulates. Solvent vapour goes straight through them, and the false
sense of protection is actively worse than wearing nothing — people stay in the booth longer.

Organic vapour needs a **cartridge respirator**, fitted and sealed, and the right cartridge for
the solvent. See the [spray booth](../tools/workshop/spray-booth.md).
:::

## Respirators only work if they fit

A respirator seals against skin. It does not seal through stubble, and it does not seal on a
face a different shape from the one it was bought for.

:::info[Needs a real answer]
Whether the space provides respirators or expects members to own a fitted one, and whether
there is any fit-testing.

There is a real decision here, not just a blank: a shared respirator that fits nobody in
particular is close to useless, and a space that provides one is implicitly claiming it
protects people. Most spaces land on "we provide cartridges, you own the mask". Someone needs
to decide and write it down.
:::

## Looking after it

- **Scratched or crazed eye protection is not eye protection.** Report it, and it gets replaced.
  Don't put it back on the hook.
- **Don't clean polycarbonate with solvent.** It crazes and then you cannot see out of it.
- **Note the date you open a respirator cartridge.** They have a working life once the seal is
  broken, whether or not you use them.
- **Shared PPE stays in the building.** Yours to use, not yours to take.
