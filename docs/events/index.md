# Events

**Every event builds something.** Real devices, real knowledge transfer. That is the rule the
programme is designed around, and it is why there are no talks with slides and a Q&A.

## The pages

- **[Bench sessions](./bench-sessions.md)** — the flagship series. Six a year, included in
  membership.
- **[Recurring nights](./recurring-nights.md)** — the regular, lower-key things.
- **[Propose an event](./propose-an-event.md)** — how to run one.

## Why "we build something" is the rule

A hackspace fills up with spectators if you let it. A talk draws forty people who watch and go
home; a session where everyone leaves holding a board they flashed draws twelve people who come
back.

It also changes who proposes events. "I will explain how X works" is easy to pitch and hard to
deliver well. "Twelve people will each leave with a working X" forces you to think about
materials, bench space, and what can go wrong — which is the thinking that makes an evening
good.

Consequence worth stating plainly: **if you turn up to watch, you will be handed a soldering
iron.** See [Start here](../start-here/index.md).

## Guests

Members may bring **one guest** to a bench session. Outside sessions, guests are arranged in
advance in the members' Telegram.

A guest is your responsibility from the door: your induction does not cover them, they do not
touch an inducted machine, they leave when you do. See
[How to get in](../start-here/how-to-get-in.md).

## What is not an event

**The space cannot be hired.** Not for a meetup, not for a launch, not for a company away-day.
Members propose events; the space does not take bookings. This is on the landing page and it is
the most common inbound request.
