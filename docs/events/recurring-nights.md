# Recurring nights

Smaller than a [bench session](./bench-sessions.md): no host, no build kit, no slot in the
six-a-year programme. Just a night when people who want to do a particular thing are reliably
in the building.

Low effort to start and the thing that actually makes a space feel inhabited.

## Ones that work in most spaces

Offered as a starting point, not a plan:

- **Repair night.** Bring something broken. Half of it gets fixed, and you learn more in an
  evening than a month of tutorials.
- **Bring-up night.** Everyone brings a board that does not work yet and the room debugs them in
  turn. Uses the [bench](../tools/electronics/index.md) for exactly what it is for.
- **Reverse-engineering night.** One target, everyone's tools, your own hardware only — see
  [Responsible use](../flipper/responsible-use.md).
- **Soldering clinic.** Someone good at it, a few people who want to be. Fixes the skill that
  gates everything else.
- **Open bench.** No theme. Doors open, benches available, people turn up. Often the best
  attended thing a space does.
- **Documentation night.** Deeply unglamorous. An evening of people writing up their
  [project pages](../projects/index.md) and fixing [tool pages](../tools/index.md) is worth more
  to the wiki than any amount of good intentions.

## Starting one

Lower bar than a bench session:

1. **Pick a night and commit to it for two months.** The first two are quiet. The fourth is not.
   Most recurring nights die because they were cancelled when three people came.
2. **Post it in the members' Telegram**, and add a row above.
3. **Say what it is not**, so people know whether to come. "Bring something broken" filters
   better than "electronics night".
4. **Turn up yourself**, every time. The night is the person who runs it, until it isn't.

No approval needed as long as you are not booking the whole space, spending money, or inviting
non-members. Then it is a [proposal](./propose-an-event.md).
