# Projects

Member projects, one page each, from a [shared template](./project-template.md).

The wall in the lobby is for finished things. This is for things in progress — which is most
things, most of the time, and the more useful record.

## Why bother writing one

Three reasons, in the order they actually pay off:

1. **Someone else has your problem.** The space has twelve benches and a Telegram; a project
   page is how the other person working on brushless control finds you instead of both of you
   solving it separately.
2. **You will forget.** The reason you chose that part, the setting that made the reflow work,
   the thing that broke twice. Six months later the page is the only copy.
3. **It is how a bench session gets proposed.** A project page with a problem in it is a better
   event pitch than an idea — see [Propose an event](../events/propose-an-event.md).

## Starting one

From your browser, no checkout: copy the **[project template](./project-template.md)**, then
**[open a new file in `docs/projects/`](https://github.com/ashelomentsev/flipperhackspacewiki/new/main/docs/projects)** and paste it. The sidebar is generated
from the folder tree, so it appears on its own — then add a row to the table above.

New to this? [Editing this wiki](../contributing.md) is the two-minute version.

A page is worth starting when you have **a goal and a first problem**. Not when it works. A page
that only appears once a project succeeded is a page that hid all the interesting parts.

## What belongs on a project page

- What you are making and why. Two sentences is enough.
- Where it has got to, honestly, including the bit that does not work.
- What you would like help with. **This is the part people read.**
- Decisions and why, so future-you can argue with past-you.
- Photos of it going wrong. Far more useful than photos of it going right.

## What does not

- **Anything from [Responsible use](../flipper/responsible-use.md)** that the policy rules out.
  A project page is public — this wiki builds to the open web.
- **Credentials, keys, door codes, card dumps.** Nothing from
  [Editing this wiki](../contributing.md)'s not-on-the-wiki list.
- **Other members' faces or unreleased work** without asking them.
- **A commercial pitch.** Residents build products here and that is fine; the project page is
  for the engineering, not the funding round.

## Projects that go quiet

They do, and that is normal — most of them are someone's evenings. A page that has not moved in
a year is still useful: it records what was tried.

Mark it `Dormant` in the status rather than deleting it. If you have abandoned it and someone
else could take it on, say so. That is how a space full of half-finished things becomes an
advantage rather than a graveyard.
