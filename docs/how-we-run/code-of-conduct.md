# Code of conduct

**Binding on members, guests, hosts and staff**, everywhere the space operates — the building,
the members' Telegram, and events.

## The short version

The space is full of people who are better than you at something and worse than you at something
else, and everyone is here to build. Act like it.

## What is expected

- **Assume competence.** Someone who has not used a machine is not someone who cannot learn it.
  The person asking a basic question may know more than you about the next thing.
- **Help when asked, don't take over.** Hands off someone else's board unless invited. Showing
  someone is better than doing it for them.
- **Respect the tools and the next person.** The bench you leave is someone's starting point.
- **Take correction well.** If someone says you are about to damage something or hurt yourself,
  stop first and discuss after.
- **Give correction well.** Privately, about the thing, once.
- **Ask before photographing people** or their unreleased work. See
  [House rules](../start-here/house-rules.md).

## What is not acceptable

- **Harassment** in any form: sexual, or based on race, religion, gender, gender identity or
  expression, sexual orientation, disability, age, body size, appearance or nationality.
- **Unwelcome sexual attention**, comments or imagery.
- **Deliberate intimidation**, stalking, or following someone around the building.
- **Sustained disruption** of events, conversations or someone's work.
- **Publishing someone's private information** without consent.
- **Condescension as a hobby.** Gatekeeping, "actually", and explaining someone's own
  speciality to them. This is the most common failure in hardware spaces and the one most often
  excused.
- **Pressuring someone to drink, to stay, or to keep working** when they want to stop.
- **Retaliation** against someone who reported something.

## Reporting

:::info[Needs a real answer — and it is the most important blank on this page]
**There is no named person and no non-Telegram route yet.** A code of conduct without a way to
report is decoration, and the group chat is not a reporting channel: the most likely person to
need this route is someone reporting a member who is in it.

Three things needed:

- **A named individual**, with a direct email that is not a shared inbox the whole membership
  reads.
- **A second route** for when the complaint concerns that person.
- **What happens next**: who investigates, what interim measures exist, how the reporter is kept
  informed.

Needs settling by Flipper Technologies Ltd before the space takes its first member.
:::

Until it is answered: [hackspace@flipper.net](mailto:hackspace@flipper.net), which is read by
the company rather than the membership, or any space lead in person.

## How a report is handled

Based on what the space has committed to; the process above still needs filling in.

1. **You will be believed and taken seriously.** Being asked clarifying questions is not doubt.
2. **No action is taken without telling you**, unless someone's immediate safety requires it.
3. **Confidentiality as far as possible.** Acting on a report usually means the other person
   learns something was said; you will be told before that happens.
4. **Outcomes range** from a conversation, to being asked to leave an event, to
   [membership withdrawn](./membership.md#withdrawal). Serious breaches act immediately.
5. **Retaliation is itself a breach**, treated as seriously as the original.

## If you are on the receiving end of a complaint

Listen first. Most things resolve with "I didn't realise, I'll stop" and genuinely end there.
Arguing that no offence was intended addresses a point nobody made.

## For hosts

Running an [event](../events/propose-an-event.md) makes you the first person people come to. Two
things:

- **Say the code applies** at the start. Twenty seconds.
- **You can stop an event.** Any member can; as host you especially can, and nobody will be
  second-guessed for it.
