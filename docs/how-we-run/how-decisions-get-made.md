# How decisions get made

## The short version

**Flipper Technologies Ltd has the final say.** It holds the lease, owns the kit and sets the
prices. This is a company-run space, not a co-operative — see [How we run](./index.md).

Within that, a lot is genuinely devolved, and more than the sentence above suggests.

## Who decides what

| Decision | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Prices, tiers, membership terms | Flipper Technologies Ltd |
| Who gets a membership, and withdrawal | Flipper Technologies Ltd |
| What kit gets bought | Flipper Technologies Ltd, usually on members' suggestions |
| The lease, the building, the hours | Flipper Technologies Ltd |
| Safety rules and induction requirements | Space leads, with the tool's maintainer. **Not up for a vote** |
| Whether a machine is fit to use | Its maintainer. Final, and nobody overrules it |
| How a tool is set up, stocked and documented | Its maintainer |
| Whether an event runs | [Propose an event](../events/propose-an-event.md) — mostly the proposer |
| Starting a [recurring night](../events/recurring-nights.md) | You. No approval |
| What goes on this wiki | Any member — see [Editing this wiki](../contributing.md) |
| Bench conventions, where things live, how the lab works day to day | The people using it |

## Where members actually decide things

Worth spelling out, because the honest framing above can read as "members decide nothing":

- **Tool maintainership.** A maintainer's call on their machine is final. That is real authority
  over the thing members touch most.
- **This wiki.** Nearly every page, no review. Including the one you are reading.
- **Events and nights.** You can start a recurring night this week without asking anyone.
- **How the space feels.** Whether benches get left clean, whether the condescension in the
  [code of conduct](./code-of-conduct.md) actually gets called out, whether a session that drifts
  gets stopped. None of that is decidable from an office.

## Where safety is not a democracy

Safety rules, induction requirements and a maintainer's decision that a machine is out of service
are **not** consensus decisions and not subject to appeal by popularity.

This is deliberate. The failure mode in member-run spaces is a safety rule getting eroded by a
majority who find it inconvenient — usually the people who have used the machine most and feel
the rule is for beginners. The rule survives the vote here because there is no vote.

You can argue a safety rule is wrong. Do it with the maintainer and a space lead, with a reason.
Until it changes, it is the rule.

## How to raise something

1. **Talk to whoever owns it** from the table above. Most things end here.
2. **Post in the members' Telegram** if it affects everyone — tool conventions, consumables,
   something the wiki gets wrong.
3. **Email [hackspace@flipper.net](mailto:hackspace@flipper.net)** for anything about money,
   terms, or that you would rather not put in a group chat.
4. **A [code of conduct](./code-of-conduct.md) concern** has its own route, which is deliberately
   not the group chat.

## Open questions

:::info[Needs a real answer]
- **Who the space leads are.** Named, so "ask a space lead" means something. See
  [Who to contact](../the-space/who-to-contact.md#roles).
- **Is there a members' forum?** A monthly meeting, or a channel where things get decided rather
  than discussed. Most spaces end up needing one; this one has not opened yet.
- **How members propose kit purchases**, and whether there is a budget.
- **The appeal route for a withdrawn membership** — flagged on
  [Membership](./membership.md#withdrawal) and still open.
:::
